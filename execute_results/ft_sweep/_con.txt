ed departure times. These simplifications were
adopted to isolate the core dispatch-tracking problem, but
richer heterogeneity and operational constraints should be
incorporated in future work.
VIII. CONCLUSION
This paper proposed an aggregator-to-station hierarchical
MADDPG-based control framework for low-latency EV
VPP operation in balancing markets. By integrating two
objectives—dispatch tracking and user charging-demand
satisfaction—within a CTDE-based multi-agent formulation,
we demonstrated a control architecture in which the ag-
gregator relays the TSO dispatch signal and station-level
agents autonomously determine local charger-level charg-
ing/discharging commands from station-local observations
and the broadcast dispatch signal. The aggregate fleet re-
sponse emerges from these local decisions rather than from an
explicit online power allocation computed by the aggregator.
The case study showed that the proposed method can
achieve high dispatch-tracking performance (97% tracking
rate), but the Target SoC satisfaction rate of 80% (with an av-
erage SoC shortfall of approximately 5%) remains a clear lim-
itation that must be addressed before practical deployment.
The study also clarified that the main architectural advan-
tage of the proposed method lies in reducing aggregator-side
online computation and avoiding online global optimization,
whereas overall practical response-time feasibility must also
account for communication/control-plane latency and device-
side actuation dynamics.
These results suggest that aggregator-to-station hierarchi-
cal MARL is a promising pathway for EV VPP control when
low-latency operation and scalability make fully centralized
control less attractive.
Future work will focus on two directions: (i) approaching
near-100% user satisfaction via a hybrid controller with a
rule-based safety layer, where the actor output is overridden
to command maximum charging only when the remaining
time and current SoC indicate that meeting the Target SoC
is physically infeasible; and (ii) extending the simulation
environment to include heterogeneous EV battery capacities
and charger ratings, broader station characteristics, explicit
station-level available-power limits, switching constraints,
stochastic departures, and larger-scale fleet experiments.
